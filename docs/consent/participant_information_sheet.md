# Participant Information Sheet

### What is this study about?

Some medicine names look or sound so similar that they are confused with one
another — for example DOPamine and DOBUTamine, or Celebrex and Celexa. When a
name is handwritten, the risk increases.

We are building software that reads a handwritten medicine name and warns a
pharmacist when the reading could dangerously be one of these similar drugs.

### Why are we asking you?

We have trained our software on computer-generated handwriting, because real
prescriber handwriting is not publicly available. Computer-generated writing is
not the same as yours. Without samples of genuine prescriber handwriting, we
cannot honestly say whether the software works.

You are being asked because you are a practising prescriber.

### What would I have to do?

Write medicine names onto one or two printed sheets. Each sheet lists 22 names;
you copy each into the box beneath it.

**It takes about five minutes.** Please write at your normal speed, in your
normal hand. We are not looking for neat writing — ordinary, fast, real
prescribing handwriting is exactly what is useful to us.

That is the whole study. There is nothing else, and no follow-up.

### Will any patient information be involved?

**No.** You will not write about any patient. The sheets contain only medicine
names that we print for you to copy. Please do not write any patient's name,
age, or any other detail anywhere on the sheet.

### Will I be identified?

No. You will be given a code such as `W07`. Only that code is stored with your
handwriting. Your name appears only on the consent form, which is kept
separately from the handwriting samples, in a locked or encrypted store that
only the investigator can open.

Nothing published from this study will identify you.

### Are there any risks?

We do not expect any. You are writing words on paper. You will not be assessed,
scored, or compared with anyone, and your handwriting will not be judged.

Handwriting can in principle be recognisable, which is why your samples are
stored under a code rather than your name.

### Are there any benefits?

Not directly to you. The intended benefit is to patients: better detection of
dangerous drug-name confusion before a medicine is dispensed.

### Do I have to take part?

No. Taking part is entirely voluntary, and declining has no consequences of any
kind.

You may stop at any point, including partway through a sheet, without giving a
reason. You may also withdraw afterwards — tell the investigator your writer
code and all of your samples will be deleted. After the data has been published
or irreversibly anonymised, withdrawal may no longer be possible; the
investigator will tell you the date after which this applies.

### What happens to the samples?

They are used to test and improve the software. They may form part of a dataset
described in a student project report, a preprint, or a published paper. If any
data is shared publicly, only the small cropped images of individual words and
their labels would be released — never the full sheets, and never your name.

### Important

This software is a research prototype. **It is not a medical device, has not
been clinically evaluated, and is not used in any patient's care.** Your
participation has no effect on any patient.
